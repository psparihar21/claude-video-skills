# Formats

Use the section that matches `format`. Ignore the others.

Shared rule: the voice knows the ending and does not announce the structure. No "firstly, secondly", no "point number three" unless the video is visibly a numbered list and the number is the design.

## faceless-explainer

Finance, science, history, product, "things you didn't notice", listicles. B-roll and text cards. No host.

- Open on the odd fact or the costly mistake, in the first sentence.
- Each card gets one implication, then silence if the card is held to be read.
- Numbers come from the card or the brief. The voice interprets them. "₹2 lakh a year" on screen can become "that is the part people treat as small" and must not become a different figure.
- Tone is a sharp friend who looked it up, not a teacher and not a broker. No "you should invest", no guaranteed returns, no medical instructions. You may say what the video shows people doing.
- Chapter breaks in a longform edit get one fresh sentence, not a recap of the whole video.

## faceless-story

A story over footage that is not the point. The listener should be able to follow it with the screen minimized.

- Present tense. One protagonist, named only if the brief named them. Otherwise "he", "she", or "yeh banda".
- A beat every shot change: new information, not a restatement.
- No moral until the edit's payoff shot. Do not preview the lesson in the hook. The hook is the trouble.
- Dialogue inside the story is reported, short, and rare. "She tells him to wait" rather than a performed two-hander. You have one voice.

## faceless-gameplay

The match is the subject. Commentary, not a let's-play and not a cast of every input.

Say:

- the decision ("he wide-swings again")
- the stake ("one more death and the rank is gone")
- the turn ("third party from the stairs")
- the outcome in plain words

Do not say:

- button paths, crosshair bragging, "oh my god" on a loop
- patch trivia you were not given
- the player's real name or rank unless it is on screen or in the brief

Leave air on the frag, the death screen, and the clutch pop. The sound of the game does that moment better than a sentence. Start the next line after it.

If the edit is a montage, write to the montage's idea (bad rotates, one gun, solo queue), not to each kill as a new story.

## story-over-gameplay

The common Indian faceless shape: a story, a confession, a fact thread, played over Subway Surfers, GTA driving, Minecraft parkour, satisfying gameplay.

- Write the story as in `faceless-story`.
- Mention the gameplay in a cue only when the beat map shows a visual gag that hits the same beat as the sentence. One nod is a lot. Two is the maximum for a short.
- Do not explain the game. The viewer did not come for it.
- Keep the voice dry. The gameplay is already loud.

## Platform

| Platform | Open | Pace | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `youtube-shorts`, `reels`, `tiktok` | Speech by 0.35s, role `hook` | 2.3–2.6 wps, sentences under 14 words | One idea. Payoff, then optional end card in silence |
| `youtube-long` | Speech by 5s, or a marked silent open | 2.2–2.5 wps | Cold open, then follow the edit's chapters. No mid-roll begging |
| `instagram-feed` | Treat as a short if under 60s, else as longform with a faster open | | |

If the platform is missing, use the duration. Under 60 seconds follows the short rules.
