# video-edit RULES

RULES.md overrides SKILL.md. Read this file at the start of every run.
Add new rules at the bottom, one per correction, dated, in the user's words. Remove a rule only when the user says to remove that rule.

## Starter rules (2026-10-14)

- All 7 tools are required. Name any skip.
- SnipSound is the only music and effect source.
- Parakeet timestamps drive cuts and captions.
- Wait for "yes" on edit-decision.md before the final render.
- Tech long form is 16:9. Shorts are 9:16.
- Gameplay HUD stays fully visible during action.
- Captions stay clear of the facecam.
- Keep a license line for every audio file.
- Do not invent product facts. Chrome sources only.

## My style (chosen by the user, 2026-10-14)

- Quality bar (2026-10-15): "high prod quality video and motion graphic". Every video and every graphic is made to pro YouTube standard. Render at the source resolution and frame rate, hard cuts only, no stray frames, no placeholder text, every graphic checked frame by frame before delivery.
- Graphic look (2026-10-15): flat Glacier White cards with black type, rounded corners, a thick black left edge, Segoe UI Black for values and small spaced caps for labels. No neon, no gaming HUD, no 3D spin. Cards sit top-left, slide in from the left with an ease-out (0.35 s) and slide back out (0.3 s), and never cover the product, hands or box.
- Graphic set (2026-10-15): section tags (01 · THE BOX, 02 · INSIDE, ...), a name card, one spec card per part (label + big value + detail lines), a battery card with a battery icon, a price card with a SALE DEAL label, an end line, and a SUBSCRIBE pill on the final freeze.
- Camera feel (2026-10-15): slow push-in 1.0 to about 1.06 on every shot, 1.08 to 1.1 on spec shots, pushed toward the product side so the left stays clear for cards. Punch-ins 1.3 to 1.5 for the funny end. Never shake.
- Hook (2026-10-15): deadpan joke first, held long enough to be believable. If the clean frames are short, freeze the last clean frame with a slow push-in. Hook stays under 3 s. Name card lands on the payoff.
- Music (2026-10-15): one low SnipSound bed under the whole video, about -27 LUFS on its own, fades in 0.8 s and out 1.8 s, ducks under natural sound. Keep room for the user's voice added later. Export the music and the natural-sound stems separately so the user can rebalance.
- Natural sound (2026-10-15): keep the real sound of moments the user names (paper rip, a funny reaction). Everything else is music plus the new voice.
- Ending (2026-10-15, replaced same day): no funny-moment ending unless the user asks for it again; it was removed for being poorly done. End on the product hero shot with the end line, then a 0.5 s fade out with the music tail.
- Intro and outro (2026-10-29, v2 replaced the tape pack the same day): the Pushpstation gaming pack on every video (`brand/`), with navy, violet and yellow slash shapes like a pro gaming channel. Hook first, then a 2.9 s sting; slash transitions at section changes; a subscribe click on the sign-off; long videos end on a 15 s branded end screen, Shorts on a 3.6 s full-frame end card. Smooth motion, never a wobble.
- Pace and transitions (2026-10-15): fast. Average shot about 1.5 s, cuts on the music beat grid (detect BPM, cut on beat multiples). Cuts are hard cuts with a zoom-punch (about 10% in and out over 0.15-0.2 s). Section changes get a light sweep, reveals get a 3-frame white flash, both drawn in the HyperFrames overlay. No wipes, no shake.
- Finish quality (2026-10-15): light denoise (hqdn3d), medium contrast curve, saturation 1.1, mild unsharp, lanczos scaling. Graphics are rendered by HyperFrames as one transparent 4K WebM and composited once. Final 4K at NVENC cq 16, plus a 1080p copy at cq 17 that plays on any device.
- Cut accuracy (2026-10-16): find a named action by its sound as well as its picture (high-pass energy spike) and view frames every 0.2 s before cutting; use only the real sound of that action, never the other recorded sound. Soften an unavoidable jump with a 10-frame dissolve or a tighter second framing, not a bare repeat. A zoom must follow its subject (track it), never a fixed corner. End every video on a shot where the product is still in frame.
- Production check (2026-10-16): before delivering, render a frame just before and just after every cut and inspect them for jumps, zoom resets across continuous footage, a person or camera man at the frame edge, graphics over the product, and blank starts. Run blackdetect, freezedetect and loudness. Drop a requested idea completely when the user says so (paper tear and its sound were removed).
- Voice and music (2026-10-16): the voice is rendered with own-voice-render from a timed cue list; picture and graphics are retimed to the voice, never the other way round. For expressiveness, pick livelier reference clips of the user's own voice (measure pitch range), restrict to two or three references for consistency, add "!" where the voice should lift, and lock the chosen takes. Music is a story arc of matching tracks joined with 3 s crossfades (curious unboxing, change at the reveal, optimistic close), ducked under the voice with sidechain compression, final mix about -14 LUFS with true peak at or below -2.2 dB before AAC so it stays under -1.5 dB after encoding.
- Benchmark screens (2026-10-16): CPU and GPU get their own full screens. CPU: Cinebench 2024 and Geekbench 6 with range bars and the median. GPU: 3DMark Time Spy plus FPS bars for major games with a 60 fps line. Always label published or estimated figures and name the source; never present them as measured on the user's laptop.
- Web page scenes (2026-10-16): frame the zoom so the product picture, title and price all stay inside the window for the whole shot; put cards where they cover none of them.
- Script (2026-10-15): always deliver a voiceover script file with a start time and delivery note per line, in Hinglish with English letters, about 2.4 words per second, spec names and numbers exact, a gap left for natural sound, and no voice over the funny ending. Also deliver a silent picture-lock so the user can re-edit after recording.
- Language: "Hindi or Hinglish". Hindi/Hinglish speech is transcribed with GPU Whisper large-v3; English-only speech uses Parakeet. Whichever engine ran, its word timestamps drive cuts and captions.
- Pacing: "Balanced". Remove dead air, ums and retakes; keep natural breathing pauses.
- Captions: "Clean minimal". Small clean line at the bottom, no animation, no word highlight.
- Caption letters: "English letters ("yeh video dekho")". Hindi/Hinglish captions and SRT are in Roman Hinglish, never Devanagari. English loanwords use normal English spelling (video, settings, click).
- DaVinci Resolve: "Only when really needed". Otherwise FFmpeg does the edit.
