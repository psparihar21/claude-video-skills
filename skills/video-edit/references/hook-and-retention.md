## What gaming channels actually do
- Big channels have **no logo intro**. Techno Gamerz and jacksepticeye start on gameplay or talking at frame 1. MortaL's 8 s glowing logo reveal (2019) is the old template look; avoid it.
- Branding is a 1-3 s sting **after** the hook, or a permanent small mark.
- Endings: a fast transition into a 15-20 s end screen (Techno Gamerz swoosh into key art; Total Gaming a red template with "THANKS FOR WATCHING"). Techno Gamerz also runs a red SUBSCRIBE button that a cursor clicks (it turns grey SUBSCRIBED and the bell rings) during the sign-off. Our v4 pack follows that grammar in its own cobalt, volt and coral look.

## The opening (most important 30 s)
1. **Cold open = the best moment first.** Start the clip just before the peak (the kill, the crash, the reveal), never on setup. Up to 5-8 s of the strongest clips, then the brand sting, then the video.
2. **It must work muted.** YouTube's hover preview plays without sound, so the first frames need visible action or a visual surprise, not a face saying "hi".
3. **Prove the title and thumbnail in the first 5 s.** Show the thing the thumbnail promised, or tease it on screen.
4. **Open a loop, close it late.** State the question or the stake early ("is it worth Rs 4,999?", "can I beat this boss?") and pay it off later. About every 60 s ask: what is the viewer wondering right now, and can the answer wait a little longer?
5. **Edit the first 30 s hardest and fastest.** Most viewers leave there. Plan it shot by shot before cutting (a paper storyboard is fine). After about 60% of the video, effort can drop.
6. **No greeting before the hook.** Matches the style card: news or reaction first, "Pushpstation pe swagat hai" after.

## Pacing
- **Match the density to why people watch.** Commentary gameplay is "hanging out with Pushp": cut dead air, keep reactions, few effects. News and lists can run faster with more graphics. Never MrBeast-level effects on a calm video; over-editing irritates.
- **Change what is on screen every few seconds** by switching between gameplay, facecam, poster, card and zoom, not by cutting for the sake of cutting. A shot that stays interesting can stay 10 s.
- **Jump cuts leave no gap.** The last syllable runs straight into the next first syllable; if it sounds clipped, add one frame to the incoming side.
- **Every sequence is motivated.** No slow-motion, montage or drone-style shots unless the story needs them.
- **Reaction cutaways.** On a big moment, cut to the facecam reaction, then back.
- **Keep the eye in one place across a cut.** If the action was at the right of frame, the next shot's subject starts near the right.
- **Guide the eye.** Zoom toward the thing being talked about, darken or blur around it, or ring it with a bracket or circle.
- **Stills move.** Any poster or screenshot gets a slow push (already in My style); a punch-in on the line that matters.

## Graphics
- **One look everywhere.** One font set, one colour set, one or two transitions repeated through the whole video. For Pushpstation that is the v4 gaming pack (`brand/`): controller-shape transitions, extruded Unbounded type, drifting name walls.
- **Nothing pops in silently.** Every graphic enters with motion (slash, rise, chip grow) or a sound (whoosh, impact, tick).
- **Text lands with the voice.** On-screen words appear on the frame the word is spoken.
- **Do the fun animation last.** Finish the whole cut first, then spend spare time on showpiece graphics.

## Sound
- A sound on every movement (whoosh), appearance (slap/pop) and highlight. Use risers only right before a real payoff, and hits to land it. Overused, they stop working.
- Music follows the mood of each section. Change the track or its energy at a topic shift. Cut the music suddenly to spotlight a moment, and fade it out to signal that a section is ending. Line up a musical lift with the switch from problem to answer.

## Ending
- Keep the sign-off short and go straight into the 15 s end screen (YouTube allows 5-20 s): two video slots plus subscribe, no more. Avoid a long "thanks for watching" tail.
- Shorts: no intro sting before the action (the NOW PLAYING band rides on top of the hook). End on real footage with the "full video on the channel" overlay; an ending that flows back into the start helps replays.
